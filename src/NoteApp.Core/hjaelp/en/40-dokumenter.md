# Documents and meeting types

*The minutes, the task list — and how you decide what goes in them*

Select a recording that has been transcribed and press **Create document**
above the transcript. Then you choose a meeting type, and the document is
saved as a Word file.

## The documents sit with the recording they came from

On **Recordings** the content pane has a tab called **Documents**. It shows
the documents made from the recording you have selected — with date, meeting
type and language model. The number on the tab says how many there are, so you
can see it without clicking.

From here you can open a document in Word. To move, rename or delete one,
**All documents** at the bottom leads to the archive, where every document is
gathered — including those from other recordings. The archive has no menu
entry of its own: you get there from here, from a search, from the history or
from the notice that a document is ready.

The link holds even if you rename the recording or move it to another folder.
It goes by the meeting's id, not by its name.

## The meeting type decides the content

A meeting type is the rules for what the document must contain, how long it
may be, and what must not appear in it.

If the meeting type was chosen when the recording was started, it is selected
in advance.

You can make your own under **Meeting types**. Either from an empty one, or by
answering a few questions and letting the language model write it — and then
correcting it afterwards.

## Tick what should be included

The ordinary wish is not to rewrite an instruction. It is to get a section in
or out.

So the sections are **tick boxes** on the «How it should be written» tab — one
per heading in the text below. Untick «Quotations», and no quotations appear
in the document. If you make a meeting type with sections of your own, they
are there by themselves; the list is read from the text and not from a list
inside the app.

The same goes for the participant rules — who ends up on the list of
participants at all — and for the fields on the «What is included» tab: the
meeting's text, your notes, the title, the date. If a field is not ticked, the
information does not go along, whatever the rules ask for.

**The text stays.** A tick box that deleted the section would be a deletion
you cannot undo. Instead the deselected lines are **struck through** where they
stand, while you click — so you can see exactly what is going, and put the
tick back.

The last tick cannot be removed. A document without a single section is not a
document.

## How long the document may be

There is no longer a number for temperature or for tokens on the screen. Those
are words from the world of language models, and for what the app does there
is only one sensible answer. The value is still in the file and is used
unchanged; you are simply not asked about it.

Instead the length is chosen in pages: short at about one page, ordinary at
two or three, long at five, very long at ten — or as long as necessary.

It is a **ceiling, not a target**. If the meeting was short, the document
comes out short, whatever it says here.

## The agenda builds itself

The **Agenda** tab shows what the meeting has to cover. It is built from the
sections you have ticked, every time the tab is drawn. Remove a tick and the
item disappears at once.

There is no longer a saved agenda. It could fall out of step with the sections
without saying so, and then you spent time in the meeting on something nobody
was going to write down. Now the chain runs one way: tick a section, the
agenda gets an item, the meeting says it out loud, and the document has a
section to write it in.

Three items are fixed: the round of names, the purpose, and the wrap-up.
Without the round of names, speech recognition has no names to recognise, and
then «who said what» becomes a guess.

**Copy the agenda** puts the whole text on the clipboard so it can go into the
meeting invitation. Minutes can only contain what was said out loud.

## The place the text is sent out

Unlike the transcription, this does not happen on the machine. The
transcript's **text** is sent to Mistral AI in France. The audio stays put.

Never the audio. Never your notes, unless the meeting type expressly asks for
them.

Every transmission is recorded under **Compliance**: time, address, model, how
many characters went, what it cost — and a checksum. Also when the call fails.

## Read it through before you pass it on

The model can get who said what wrong — especially if the names were never
said out loud in the meeting. It has previously added a participant who was
not there.

Minutes can only contain what was said. If a decision is never spoken out
loud, it does not appear in the document either, however good the model is.

## Redo it

The transcript stays with the recording and can be used again. If the minutes
did not come out as you wanted, you make a new set from the same text with a
different meeting type — as many times as you like.

The old document stays until you delete it yourself.

## The short summary

The transcript screen also holds a **short summary**: ten lines about what the
meeting was about, what was agreed, and what was left open. It is there to
remember the meeting by — not a document you have to name and save.

It is not made on the machine. The transcript's text goes the same way as for
a document, so it needs both a key and a connection. In return it only happens
when you press the button — never because you clicked on a tab.
